Draft

The 1st of July marked the date at which I started my job as data
steward with the Global Health Engineering group at ETH Zurich. Hired as
an Open Science Specialist, a role and job description we defined our
self, I can now look back to five years of experience providing research
data support in practice. I am embedded in the group, have developed a
data management strategy with a foundation in Open Science and Open
Research Data, supervised five funded projects as part of the ETH Domain
Open Research Data funding, and am developing strategies for measuring
the impact and value of my services.

I strongly argue for data stewards to be embedded at the level of the
research group, to support already overburdened professors with the ever
evolving word of research data and research software management. Data
stewardship requires an approach that is inherently personal, as every
member of a research group comes with their unique skills and
competencies, which require targeted coaching. Our vision is not for
everyone to apply the same tools and workflows to their research, but to
develop a mental shift towards a research culture that is open,
transparent and conducted with rigour which places computational
reproducibility at the core of the researches mindset.

In this talk, I will share my view of data stewardship and how we at the
Global Health Engineering group use our internal data to better
understand the impact of our practices. I will highlight our work on the
ghedata data package, which openly shares the data we collect to develop
strategies for better Research Intellegince.

Description ghedata paper: Ghedata paper

As part of the overlapping work for the group and the Open Science team,
we decided—at Lars’ request—to document our collective work as Data
Stewards in a paper. More specifically, the paper focuses on gaining
'research intelligence' for the team (analogous to business
intelligence) by using not only our research outputs but operational
data in general to generate actionable insights. During my final months
with GHE.

The foundation for the paper consisted of various data sources that we
compiled in a GitHub repository. These include our supervision table,
LinkedIn statistics, and metadata from Zenodo, as well as usage
statistics from GitHub. The common denominator of these sources is that
they are part of a larger picture of our operational data which, once
interconnected, we intend to use to make informed strategic decisions.
